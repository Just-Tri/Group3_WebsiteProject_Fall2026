History of Video Games


A video game is an interactive electronic game played using an input device to generate visual feedback on a screen. From the vast, persistent online world of The Elder Scrolls Online bringing thousands of players together in a single universe, to the hyper-realistic visual fidelity of Cyberpunk 2077, modern gaming pushes network infrastructure and visual hardware to limits once thought unthinkable. Today's interactive experiences rely on complex servers and billions of calculations per second to connect global communities in real time, yet this massive medium traces back to a far simpler era of technology.

The history of video games stretches back to the late 1950s, a period when computers were massive, room-sized machines used almost exclusively for scientific research and military defense. During an open house at the Brookhaven National Laboratory on October 18, 1958, nuclear physicist William Higinbotham sought a way to make the facility's scientific exhibits more engaging for visitors. Leveraging an analog computer designed to calculate missile trajectories and a five-inch oscilloscope screen, Higinbotham designed Tennis for Two; a simple simulation displaying a side view of a tennis court with a bouncing dot for a ball. Connected to custom-built handheld controllers featuring a dial for angle control and a button to strike the ball, it became an instant sensation, marking a foundational moment as one of the very first interactive electronic games created purely for human entertainment.

Tennis for Two (1958) by Physicist William Higinbotham ▼

Created in 1958 by physicist William Higinbotham for Brookhaven National Laboratory, Tennis for Two is widely considered the first electronic game built purely for entertainment. Played on a five-inch oscilloscope screen powered by an analog computer, two players used custom aluminum controllers with knobs and buttons to hit a bouncing point of light back and forth over a simulated net.

After Tennis for Two (1958), the next major landmark in video game history was Spacewar!, created in 1962 by Steve Russell and a team at MIT on a DEC PDP-1 computer. Allowing two players to maneuver spaceships around a central star while shooting at one another, it became the first digital computer game to spread across multiple academic institutions and serves as a vital bridge to the commercial video game industry.

Spacewar! (1962) by Steve Russell & MIT Team ▼

Developed in early 1962 using the DEC PDP-1 mainframe computer at MIT, Spacewar! featured custom built dual-joystick controls that enabled two players to battle each other in deep space. Written onto paper tape by Steve Russell, Martin Graetz, and Wayne Wiitanen, the game was freely distributed to computer labs across the country, serving as the blueprint for early digital gaming and inspiring the very first commercial arcade video games.
